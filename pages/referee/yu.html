What to emphasize

The letter is most valuable on evaluation integrity: the temporal-modeling discipline of the E-116 forecasting work and a validation design built to resist self-deception. Concrete examples from the early-window ICU mortality project carry the weight here — the leakage-safe window construction, the like-for-like model comparison, the hospital-grouped out-of-sample validation, and the calibration and drift diagnostics that closed the study. The biology is not your burden; that signal comes from the preprints and the other letters.

  • Temporal design: turning time-stamped vitals and laboratory observations into leakage-safe 6, 12, and 24 hour prediction windows, which is where most of the real difficulty lived.
  • Honest model comparison: static, dynamic, PCA-factor, and nonlinear reduced-form models compared on the same footing rather than a single model reported favorably.
  • Validation integrity: hospital-grouped out-of-sample validation across 186 hospitals, with the 24 hour result read against the base rate rather than in isolation.
  • Calibration and drift: calibration and drift diagnostics persisted alongside the metrics, not treated as an afterthought.
  • Reproducibility: the project ran inside SNPTX, so the data contract, the feature pipeline, and the metric receipts all regenerate from the notebook — the discipline behind the Snakemake + DVC + MLflow spine.

The E-116 final project you already graded supplies everything the letter needs; there is nothing new to review. This is a coursework-plus-independent-project letter rather than a supervised-research one (the ALM capstone does not begin until January 2027).
