What your letter is best positioned to establish

I didn't come up through a conventional lab, so my recommendation letters carry the research signal directly. Yours anchors the data-mining and reproducibility case, scoped to the E-108 final project you graded.

The letter lands best when it stays close to the E-108 execution: the ChEMBL-32 similarity-search and community-detection project, the data-mining design behind it, the graph analytics, and the reproducible-workflow habits that carried straight into SNPTX. The biomedical science is not your burden; that signal comes from the preprints and the other letters. What only you can add is how the work looked as it happened, and where it sits among the independent projects you've supervised.

  • Independent technical execution: the unsupervised similarity-search and community-detection project on 10,000 ChEMBL-32 molecules as 1,024-bit Morgan fingerprints, built and evaluated independently.
  • Data-mining design: exact versus approximate similarity search (FAISS / ANN / LSH), kNN similarity-graph construction, and honest method comparison (community detection versus k-means, UMAP for structure).
  • Graph analytics: community detection (greedy modularity) on the kNN graph, finding structure without labeled activity.
  • Reproducible workflow: pinned seeds, versioned data, regenerable results; the discipline behind the SNPTX Snakemake + DVC + MLflow spine.
  • Trajectory: from a strong E-108 student to single-author builder of a multi-modal biomedical ML engine.

This is a coursework-plus-independent-project letter rather than a supervised-research one. The ALM capstone you'll advise begins in January 2027, after this cycle; that assignment is fair context for the letter to note, but nothing in it has started.

Statement-of-purpose alignment. My SOP argues one research question: how to build an autonomous discovery engine whose evidence can be trusted; a system that selects, runs, and validates its own experiments without deceiving itself. Your letter speaks to the working habit that makes that question credible in my hands: results that can be regenerated, and evaluations that do not flatter the method, as you saw them in the E-108 project.
