What committees weigh Five things a selective committee actually reads for, each worth a sentence in the letter

Committees discount general praise and credit specifics with a stated reference class. Where the following are true from your vantage, saying so plainly is the most useful thing the letter can do.

  1. A comparison with a denominator. Where the student sits within the ALM Data Science and AI program against the degree's expectations and its other students (for example, a percentile or a placement among the strongest students the program has graduated). The GPA of 4.00 / 4.00 and average raw course score of 0.9955 are the record behind whatever placement you judge accurate.
  2. Readiness for doctoral research. Whether the trajectory from the foundations course to a single-author research system is one the program would expect of a student beginning a PhD, and whether you would be comfortable recommending this student to a doctoral program without reservation.
  3. Independence and initiative. A degree pursued while building, alone, a biomedical discovery system that also anchors the capstone and has produced two public preprints; the program's own view of that initiative is the one the committee wants.
  4. One concrete technical judgment. One observation from the record that you judge telling, for instance the consistency of the raw scores across seven courses, or the choice to anchor the capstone in an existing research system rather than a fresh project.
  5. Intellectual character. Sustained rigor across the degree, the maturity to carry coursework into citable research, and the standing the program is willing to put its name behind.

Statement-of-purpose alignment. My SOP argues one research question: how to build an autonomous discovery engine whose evidence can be trusted; a system that selects, runs, and validates its own experiments without deceiving itself. Your letter supplies the evidence that the person behind that question grew into it across a full degree, which is the maturity claim the statement rests on.
